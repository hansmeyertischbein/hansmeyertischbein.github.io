Market analysis: Norwegian household power retail

Norgespris has turned Norwegian household power retail into a business paid per meter, not per kilowatt hour: Elmera, the market leader, sold 16% more power to households in the first quarter of 2026 and earned 1% less for it. With volume no longer paying and switching down a fifth this year, scale and cost per customer decide earnings. That is what Fortum bought with Elmera: its EUR 30 million a year of sales and cost synergies is worth about four times a NOK 10 monthly fee rise, and is what makes the price work.

Key points

  • Extra volume no longer earns money. Elmera’s Norwegian consumer volume rose from 2,730 to 3,162 GWh in the first quarter of 2026, partly from higher use among Norgespris customers, while adjusted net revenue fell from NOK 239.6m to 238.0m [2]. Net revenue per kWh fell 14%, from 8.8 to 7.5 øre (own calculation).
  • The state sets the energy price for most households. By 31 October 2026, 1.97 million metering points, 64.6% of those eligible, were on Norgespris [12]. Retailers are left selling a monthly fee and a small mark up.
  • Customers have stopped shopping around. Supplier switches were 142,712 so far in 2026 against 178,135 in the same period of 2025, down 20%; the 2024 full year was 55% below 2021 [15][16].
  • Scale is the biggest lever left. Fortum expects about EUR 30m a year of sales and cost synergies across Elmera, fully from 2030 [6]: about NOK 320m, or 56% of Elmera’s guided 2026 EBIT. A NOK 10 rise in the Norwegian consumer fee would bring NOK 81m (own calculations).
  • The price needs those synergies. Fortum paid about 12.2 times guided 2026 EBIT, an 8.2% pre-tax return, rising to about 12.8% at full synergies from 2030, before integration costs (own calculations from [4][2][3][6]).

The market

The market is the sale of electricity supply contracts to Norwegian households and holiday homes, by retailers who buy at the Nord Pool spot price and resell with a monthly fee and a mark up per kWh. Grid tariffs are a separate, regulated charge outside it. Like Norwegian mobile services in the same theme, its story this autumn is a consolidation the regulators let through.

Norgespris is a fixed price the state settles through the grid company: a household that orders it pays 50 øre per kWh including VAT for energy, and the state covers the difference to spot [1][13]. The household still pays “the retailer’s mark-up according to their chosen supply contract” [1]. Uptake follows price: 79.6% of households in the south west (NO2) against 0.4% in the north (NO4) [12].

Volume grows, revenue does not. Household consumption rose 7.9% in the third quarter [19], and Elmera reports higher use among Norgespris customers [1]. Its revenue barely moved, because most of a retailer’s income is a fixed monthly fee and a mark up of a few øre. Norgespris is not the only cause: Elmera names “product mix changes, high elspot prices and campaign products” [2]. The data do not separate them, but they point one way: kilowatt hours, the one thing Norgespris adds, are not where the money is.

The top is concentrated, the rest fragmented. Elmera puts its consumer share at about 24% [1]. Fortum does not publish its Norwegian customer number, so this analysis cannot calculate a combined share or an HHI. Switching was falling before Norgespris, from 747,128 in 2022 to 399,881 in 2023 [16], Norgespris extends that trend: when the state fixes the energy price, there is little left to compare.

Three things follow. First, in 2025 revenue per consumer delivery fell 6.5% while cost per delivery fell 2.6%, so EBIT per delivery fell 15%; in the first quarter of 2026 cost fell faster than revenue and EBIT per delivery held at NOK 120 to 122. Cost, not price, is what the leader can still move. Second, the same company with the same systems earned NOK 395 per delivery where it had 657,000 customers in 2024 and NOK 50 where it had 118,000, in a year without the 2025 hospitality losses. Not all of that gap is scale, since the markets differ, but it shows how thin a small book is. Third, a business delivery earns six times the EBIT of a household one, which is why Business, not consumer, carried Elmera’s profit growth [1].

Investor view

Elmera was delisted on 6 November 2026 [6], so the listed exposure is Fortum (FORTUM, Nasdaq Helsinki), which now owns the Norwegian leader beside its own Norwegian business. The thesis touches its Consumer Solutions segment, EUR 213m of the group’s EUR 1,240m comparable EBITDA in 2025, or 17% [10]. Elmera’s 2026 guidance of about NOK 575m of adjusted EBIT [3] is about EUR 54m, 44% of the segment’s 2025 comparable operating profit of EUR 122m and 6% of the group’s EUR 924m (own calculation at NOK 10.7 per euro, the rate implied by Fortum’s own figures: NOK 47 for all 109.3 million shares, about EUR 482m [4][24][10][11]).

The price only works with the synergies. NOK 47 a share for 109.3 million shares is NOK 5.14bn of equity [4]. Elmera reports net interest bearing debt of NOK 1,858.9m at the end of March 2026 and NOK 1,892.8m at the end of 2025 [2]. Using March gives an enterprise value of about NOK 7.0bn (Mainsights also reports about NOK 7bn [21]), or 12.2 times guided 2026 EBIT: a pre-tax return of 8.2% (own calculations). On 6 November Fortum put its answer at about EUR 30m a year of sales and cost synergies, realised gradually from 2028 and fully from 2030 [6]. At that full run rate, before integration costs, the return is about 12.8% (own calculation); until synergies start in 2028, it stays near 8%.

The base case runs through the same numbers. If Norgespris stays for 2027 at the proposed 45 øre [13], volume keeps rising and earns little, price competition stays muted because there is little to compare, and profit moves through cost and customer count. Fortum’s synergies, about four times a NOK 10 fee rise though on a wider base, are the largest single lever in the market. That favours Fortum over regional retailers with no partner to merge back offices with, and over challengers whose growth depends on switching.

PESTEL

Effect on the earnings of the large incumbent retailers over the next two to three years.

Political

High impactMixed

Norgespris is the market’s design, and it is set each year. The government proposed 45 øre excluding VAT for 2027, several parties have said they will challenge the rise, and the Storting decides before Christmas [13][14][9]. A stable scheme keeps switching low, which helps incumbents; ending it would bring price products and switching back.

Economic

Medium impactHeadwind

High spot prices raise a retailer’s cost on campaign and fixed products without raising its fee. Elmera names high elspot prices among the reasons its consumer margin fell in the first quarter [2]. Norgespris shields the household, not the retailer.

Social

High impactTailwind

Inertia is the norm: switches fell 55% from 2021 to 2024 and are down 20% again in 2026 [16][15]. For incumbents that lowers the cost of keeping a customer and protects the largest back books, Elmera’s above all. For challengers it is a headwind.

Technological

Medium impactMixed

Apps lower cost and churn: Elmera says Fjordkraft app users have “significantly lower churn” [1], and Tibber built its model on one [20].

Environmental

Low impactHeadwind

Norgespris weakens the signal to save power: Elmera expects higher consumption per delivery and says the scheme can dampen incentives for energy efficiency [1]. For retailers that means more volume that earns little, and higher demand in the south that can lift spot prices, which Elmera says squeezes its margin [2].

Legal

Low impactTailwind

The Fortum and Elmera deal is cleared, with all regulatory approvals met on 3 September 2026 [5]. The Norgespris law requires a reference price each year [14], so the frame is stable while the level is political.

SWOT: Fortum in Norway after Elmera

Strengths

  • Owns the consumer leader, about 24% of the Norwegian market [1]
  • Elmera’s consumer business earned NOK 334 of EBIT per delivery in 2025 (own calculation, [1])
  • About EUR 13m of synergies from brand mergers in 2025 [10]

Weaknesses

  • Paid about 12.2 times guided EBIT, an 8.2% pre-tax return before synergies (own calculation)
  • Elmera’s consumer net revenue per delivery fell 6.5% in 2025 (own calculation, [1])
  • Synergies arrive only from 2028, fully from 2030 [6]

Opportunities

  • EUR 30m a year of sales and cost synergies across Elmera [6]
  • Lower switching keeps acquired customers longer [15]
  • Business deliveries earn six times the EBIT of household ones (own calculation, [1])

Threats

  • The Storting changing or ending Norgespris [14]
  • Losing customers in a brand or platform migration
  • Low mark up challengers such as Tibber at 3.40 øre/kWh [20]

Risks to the view

  1. Norgespris ends or is cut back. Without a state fixed price, households would compare price products again and switching would recover, reopening the channel for challengers. Signal: the Storting rejecting or capping it in December, or 2027 switching above the 2025 pace.
  2. Fortum loses customers in the integration. Scale only pays if the customers stay. Signal: Elmera’s consumer deliveries falling below the 679,000 of March 2026 [2] in Fortum’s reporting.
  3. Margin per kWh recovers. If spot prices fall and campaign products roll off, volume could pay again. Signal: consumer net revenue growing in line with volume for two quarters.

What to watch

  1. December 2026: the Storting’s vote on the 2027 budget, including Norgespris at 45 øre excluding VAT [13][14]. A lower price or a cap would change how much households buy.
  2. 5 to 14 January 2027: households must order Norgespris again through Elhub for it to apply from 1 January [13]. Take up below the 64.6% of 2026 would point to price comparison returning.
  3. 2 February 2027: Fortum’s financial statements bulletin for 2026 [22], the first with Elmera consolidated, and with the purchase price allocation Fortum has promised [6].
