Market analysis: Norwegian mobile services

Norway’s mobile market is going from three radio networks to two, as Telia and Ice put theirs into a joint company and leave Telenor as the only operator carrying a national network alone. The conditions the regulator Nkom attaches will decide whether this brings sharper competition or a more comfortable market with higher prices.

Key points

  • Three groups hold 90.4% of Norway’s 6.12 million mobile subscriptions: Telenor 40.4%, Telia 32.8% and Lyse, which owns Ice, 17.1% [1]. That gives an HHI of about 3,000.
  • Revenue grows through price, not volume. Mobile telephony revenue rose by NOK 1.0 billion to NOK 23.0 billion in 2025, while the number of subscriptions rose by only about 22,000 [1].
  • Telia and Lyse agreed in February 2026 to move their radio networks and spectrum into a 50/50 company, while keeping separate core networks and competing for customers [2]. Telia now expects it to be operational in the second half of 2026 [8].
  • Nkom has found no grounds so far to refuse the spectrum transfer, but says conditions may be needed, mainly because the new company would hold far more 5G spectrum in the 3.6 GHz band than Telenor until 2042 [4].
  • Norwegians pay more and use less: average revenue per subscription was NOK 305 a month in the first half of 2025, and data use was the lowest in the Nordics [11].

The market

The market here is retail mobile subscriptions for voice and data sold to consumers and businesses in Norway. It excludes machine to machine SIM cards and fixed broadband. Nkom counted 6.12 million mobile telephony subscriptions at the end of 2025, 71.3% of them private and 28.7% business [1].

The HHI adds the squared shares of Telenor, Telia, Lyse and Unifon, which comes to about 3,005. The remaining 7.5% is split among smaller providers and adds little [1]. Anything above 2,500 is usually called highly concentrated.

The market is saturated. Connections equal about 108% of the population [14], so a new customer for one operator is almost always a lost customer for another. Growth comes from charging more per subscription: average revenue per subscription, excluding machine to machine, rose from NOK 300 a month in 2024 to NOK 305 in the first half of 2025 [11]. A government commissioned comparison published in March 2026 again found Norwegian mobile and broadband prices higher than in the other Nordic countries [12].

The three network owners compete on network quality, price campaigns and bundles. Telenor charges a premium and defends it with network test results; its CEO described the second quarter as one with more promotional activity in the market [6]. Ice has grown on price and is now adding scale, while Telia is trying to turn around shrinking revenue. Coverage itself is becoming less of a weapon: once Telia and Ice share one radio network, the contest over coverage is between two networks, not three.

PESTEL

How each factor affects the three network operators over the next two to three years.

Political

Medium impactHeadwind

The government has published Nordic price comparisons since 2021, and the latest again puts Norway at the top [12]. That keeps mobile prices on the political agenda and makes further price rises harder to defend. At the same time the state has chosen to build the next emergency network on all three commercial networks, with first users planned toward the end of 2029 [13], which gives the operators a stable public partner.

Economic

High impactMixed

With subscriptions almost flat, revenue only grows through price [1]. That works while customers accept it, but costs are rising faster: Telenor’s adjusted EBITDA in Norway fell 7.7% in Q2 2026 [6] and Telia’s fell 7.0% in 2025 [7]. The pressure on margins is the main reason Telia and Ice chose to share a network.

Social

Low impactMixed

Norwegian users had the lowest mobile data use in the Nordics, 15.1 GB a month in the first half of 2025 against 19.1 GB in Sweden and 46.3 GB in Finland [11]. Higher prices are the likely reason, so cheaper large data plans could unlock more use. Habits change slowly, though, and switching between operators is driven more by campaigns than by social trends.

Technological

High impactMixed

Data traffic grew 9.9% in 2025, but 5G investment fell by NOK 853 million to NOK 2.37 billion [1]. Sharing one radio network lets Telia and Ice cover more of the country for less, with coverage gains expected by 2027 [3]. It also pools their 3.6 GHz spectrum, which gives the new company more 5G capacity than Telenor until 2042 [4]. Good for the partners, a structural disadvantage for Telenor.

Environmental

Low impactTailwind

Mobile networks run mainly on Norway’s largely renewable electricity, so emissions are not a major cost or reputational risk. Fewer parallel base stations under network sharing should reduce energy use and the number of new masts in rural areas. This matters for planning permissions, but it will not decide who wins customers.

Legal

High impactMixed

Nkom must approve the transfer of spectrum licences to the joint company, and the Competition Authority has the main role under the Competition Act [3]. Nkom calls the move from three to two radio networks a significant structural change and may set conditions. Its main worry is that providers without their own network get worse wholesale access [4][5]. Telenor’s duty to give reasonable network access is also what Ice’s roaming deal rests on [10].

SWOT: Telenor Norge

The market leader is the company most affected by the Telia and Ice network deal, since it is now the only operator building alone.

Strengths

  • Largest share, 40.4% of subscriptions [1]
  • Best rated network in 11 of 15 Opensignal categories [14]
  • Can still raise prices: mobile ARPU up 2% in Q2 2026 [6]
  • Earns wholesale revenue from Ice’s roaming, about NOK 0.4bn expected in 2026 [10]

Weaknesses

  • Losing customers: share down from 41.3% [1] and 19,000 fewer mobile subscriptions in Q2 2026 [6]
  • Adjusted EBITDA in Norway down 7.7% in Q2 2026 [6]
  • Pays for a full national network with no partner to share costs

Opportunities

  • The GlobalConnect consumer and Enivest fibre deals would lift its fibre share from about 22% to 30%, which helps bundling [6]
  • Nkom conditions on the joint company’s spectrum could limit the partners’ capacity edge [4]
  • Low Norwegian data use leaves room to sell larger plans [11]

Threats

  • A joint Telia and Ice network with more 3.6 GHz spectrum until 2042 [4]
  • Roaming revenue from Lyse falls to about NOK 0.2bn in 2027 as Ice builds its own coverage [10]
  • Political pressure on prices that are the highest in the Nordics [12]

What to watch

  1. 28 October 2026: Telenor’s third quarter results, and whether the Norwegian mobile base keeps shrinking [15].
  2. Autumn 2026: Nkom’s decision on whether to attach conditions to the spectrum transfer. The comment round closed on 7 September, and any conditions go out for a separate consultation first [5].
  3. Second half of 2026 to end of 2027: the joint Telia and Ice network going live [8], and the end of Ice’s roaming deal with Telenor after 2027 [10].
