Young adults and insurance loyalty

What would make 18–29-year-olds in Norway choose If P&C Insurance, and stay? A group market study for the course Methods and Insight at BI Norwegian Business School, spring 2026.

  • Full report (PDF, Norwegian)
  • Market concentration calculator

The question

Four companies hold 78% of the Norwegian private non-life insurance market, and If has slipped from first to second place since 2002. Industry surveys show how satisfied customers are on average, but young adults are hard to reach by phone and those surveys say little about them. Other studies show this group often doesn't know what their own policies cover.

We asked: what can If do to grow its market share and loyalty among 18–29-year-olds?

How we studied it

We started with three focus groups of 16 young adults in total, using open questions, a ranking exercise and projective techniques such as describing If as a person or a car. The themes that came up became a survey answered by 187 people aged 18–29.

From the survey we built indices for involvement, quality orientation, loyalty and view of If, each with a Cronbach's alpha of at least 0.70. We then grouped respondents with hierarchical and k-means cluster analysis, and tested differences and drivers with ANOVA, chi-square tests, t-tests and regression in JMP.

Four segments

Respondents fell into four groups that differ on how involved they are, whether they will pay for better service, and what they think of If.

Quality-conscious

27% of respondents

Moderately involved, willing to pay for faster claims handling and better service, and positive towards If.

Passive

28%

Low on everything. Insurance is something they rarely think about, and this is the only segment with clearly lower loyalty than the rest.

Price-conscious

26%

The most involved group, positive towards If, but unwilling to pay extra for quality.

Demanding

19%

Highly involved and quality-oriented, yet the most sceptical of If. This holds even among those who aren't If customers, so it reflects a real view of the brand.

What drives loyalty

Loyalty works differently depending on whether someone has filed a claim. For those who have, how satisfied they were with the claims process is the strongest driver. For those who haven't, only involvement matters: people who understand and keep track of their insurance are more loyal.

Other findings

  • Women were less involved than men and twice as likely not to know what they pay for insurance each month (24.5% against 10.6%). Men also reported slightly higher loyalty.
  • How risky people think their life is did not predict whether they would pay more for broader cover or better service.
  • If is highly visible but not clearly different. Focus group participants linked it to advertising and size, pictured it as a polished middle-aged man, and compared it to an expensive German car. Few could say what sets it apart.

What it means for If

  • The claims process is where loyalty is won or lost. Feeling believed, treated fairly and paid quickly matters more than advertising.
  • Helping young customers understand their cover builds loyalty before they ever file a claim, through clear terms and an easy app and website.
  • One message won't reach all four segments. The passive group needs to be activated, while the demanding group needs to be convinced on quality.

Limitations

The survey was shared on social media and LinkedIn, so students are overrepresented and the results can't be generalised to all young adults. Some indices rest on only two or three questions, and the demographic differences between segments were not statistically significant.
