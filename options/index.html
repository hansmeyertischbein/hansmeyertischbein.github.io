Value against the share price

Before expiry the option is worth more than its payoff because the share can still move. That extra is the time value, and it is largest when the option is at the money.

The binomial tree converges

A binomial tree lets the share go up or down each step. With more steps the European price zigzags towards the Black–Scholes price. The American price sits on or above it, because the holder can exercise early.

The first three steps of the tree

Formulas

C = S e−qT N(d1) − K e−rT N(d2)
P = K e−rT N(−d2) − S e−qT N(−d1)

d1 = [ln(S/K) + (r − q + σ²/2)T] / (σ√T),   d2 = d1 − σ√T

  • Put–call parity for European options: C − P = S e−qT − K e−rT. The page checks it on every change.
  • Binomial tree (Cox, Ross and Rubinstein): u = eσ√Δt, d = 1/u, risk-neutral probability p = (e(r−q)Δt − d)/(u − d). Work backwards from the payoffs, discounting by e−rΔt. For an American option, take the larger of that value and exercising now at every node.
  • Delta N(d1)e−qT for a call, (N(d1) − 1)e−qT for a put. Gamma e−qTN′(d1)/(Sσ√T). Vega S e−qTN′(d1)√T.

Black–Scholes assumes constant volatility and interest rates, no transaction costs and log-normal share prices. Real markets show a volatility smile, so traders quote options in implied volatility rather than price.
