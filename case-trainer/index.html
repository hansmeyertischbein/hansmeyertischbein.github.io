      How to approach a case

      1. Listen and repeat back. Restate the question and the objective in one sentence, so you solve the right problem.
      2. Clarify. Ask two or three questions that change the analysis: the goal, the time frame, what success looks like.
      3. Structure. Ask for a minute, then lay out three to five parts that do not overlap and together answer the question. Tailor it to the case, not a memorised framework.
      4. Start with a hypothesis. Say where you think the answer lies and which part you want to look at first, and why.
      5. Do the maths out loud. Round to easy numbers, keep units, and check the order of magnitude.
      6. Say what each number means. After every result, one sentence: so what for the client?
      7. Recommend. Answer first, then the reasons, risks and next steps. Commit to an answer even if the data is incomplete.
