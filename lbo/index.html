The model, year by year

Interest is charged on the debt at the start of each year, which avoids a circular model. Cash left after interest, tax, capex, working capital and scheduled amortisation is swept into early repayment, senior loan first. The model assumes no cash on the balance sheet at entry.

How to read it

  • IRR is the annual return on the equity the fund puts in. Funds typically aim for 20% or more before fees.
  • Money multiple (MoM) is equity out divided by equity in. A 2.0× over five years is about 15% a year.
  • EBITDA growth creates value at the entry multiple. Multiple expansion is selling at a higher multiple than you paid, which a careful case does not assume. Debt paydown moves value from lenders to owners as cash repays debt.
  • More debt raises the IRR when things go to plan and the risk of losing everything when they do not. Try a growth rate of −5%.

The model is simplified for teaching: annual periods, no minimum cash, no management equity, no interest limitation rule and no transaction structure. Real LBO models are monthly or quarterly for the first years and include covenants.
