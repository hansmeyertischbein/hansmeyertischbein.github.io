    How it works

    The HHI adds up the square of every company's market share, measured in percent. A market with one company scores 10,000. A market with a hundred equal companies scores 100. Squaring gives large companies far more weight than small ones, so the index reacts strongly when big players grow.

    When two companies with shares a and b merge, the index rises by 2 × a × b, assuming the combined company keeps both customer bases.

    The colour bands use common rules of thumb: below 1,000 is unconcentrated, and above 1,800 is treated as highly concentrated in the 2023 US Merger Guidelines. Thresholds differ between competition authorities, so read the label as a guide rather than a verdict.

    The example loads premium-based market shares in Norwegian non-life insurance for Q3 2025, as used in my study of young adults and insurance loyalty (source: Finans Norge).
