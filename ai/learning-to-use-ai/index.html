Learning to use AI proficiently

Most people use AI like a slot machine: type a vague request, pull the lever, hope for something good. This guide follows one episode of The Koe Cast that argues for the opposite, treating AI as an employee you train. It then applies the method to market analysis.

1The problem: AI as a slot machine

Koe's starting point is that most people stop at using AI as a new kind of search engine. They ask a question, take whatever comes back and conclude that AI is useful but not life-changing. He compares it to a slot machine: you pull the lever and hope.

His alternative is to see AI as a digital employee that does exactly what it is told. An employee given a one-line brief guesses. When AI guesses, it falls back on the average of what it has read, which is mostly mediocre advice from the internet. Koe also points out that consumer chatbots are tuned to be pleasant to the average user, so their default answers drift towards the average too.

His example is a script for a YouTube video. Ask for «a viral script about productivity» and the AI knows nothing about your format, tone, audience or the frameworks you use. Successful creators succeed because of exactly those things, so the AI has to guess at all of them.

2The core idea: narrow the context

The whole method rests on one sentence: your job is to narrow the context so the AI has to guess less. Every instruction, example and fact about your situation removes possible answers that do not fit.

That is why Koe argues for long prompts. A good prompt is not a clever phrase but a brief with several parts:

3Step 1: Teach the AI the task

If the AI does not know how a task should be done, and you cannot explain it either, the output will disappoint. So the first step is to get detailed, expert-level instructions for the task. Koe gives four ways to get them.

Two prompts for routes 3 and 4:

Below is [a chapter / an article / a transcript] by [expert] about [task].

Turn it into a step-by-step guide that someone could follow without having read the original. For each step, give:
- what to do
- why it matters
- a common mistake

My take Route 4 is the most useful for analytical work too. A strong equity research note or consulting report is a better standard than anything the AI produces by default. Use material you have the right to use, and never paste confidential work documents into a consumer chatbot.

4Step 2: Turn the instructions into a prompt

Instructions alone are still generic. They describe how to do the task, but not for you: your company, your product, your audience. Koe's answer is a metaprompt, a prompt whose only job is to write other prompts. He calls it the secret sauce.

You open a new chat, paste the metaprompt, describe the prompt you want and paste the instructions from step 1. The AI writes a finished prompt. You then run that prompt in a fresh chat, where it works in phases:

The key detail is phase 1. Instead of you guessing what context to provide, the prompt makes the AI interview you, one question at a time, until it knows enough. That is how the brief in figure 3 gets its context without you writing it.

5The method in one picture

Koe sums it up in four moves: get expert instructions, use the metaprompt to describe the prompt you want, give that prompt a context phase and an execution phase, and paste the instructions in. The result is saved, used and improved.

6Build a prompt library

Once a task has been turned into a prompt, you never have to start from zero again. Koe's advice is to document every repeatable skill this way. It lowers the mental load of starting each task, and it gives you a process you can improve, rather than a habit you repeat.

For a one-person business, Koe builds one prompt per job: a customer avatar prompt, an offer blueprint based on Alex Hormozi's material, and copywriting prompts made from classic books such as Breakthrough Advertising and Great Leads. For content, he prefers prompts that help him outline and think, not prompts that write for him.

7AI as a thinking partner

The intellectual sparring partner

Because default models are tuned to be palatable to the average person, Koe argues that learning only from them makes your thinking average too. His fix is to make the AI reason like specific clear thinkers. For each one, he has the AI write a «worldview» document: principles, way of reasoning and key ideas. He stores these as project context and asks questions through them.

Write a worldview document on [thinker], based on their own books, essays and interviews. Include:
- core principles
- how they reason about problems
- key concepts, with short definitions
- typical advice
- where critics disagree with them

Say which work each point comes from, and say when you are unsure.

My take A worldview document is the AI's paraphrase, and it can turn a thinker into a caricature. Asking for sources and for the critics' view, as in the template, helps. Reading the originals helps more.

The creative thought partner

Koe argues that good thinkers follow consistent questioning processes. His own, for writing, is six questions. He turns them into a prompt that asks one question at a time and never gives the answer, only helps you find it.

Act as a thinking coach. Help me develop an idea about [topic] by asking these questions, one at a time:
1. What is the big problem?
2. What happens if it is not solved?
3. What ideal outcome do I want to inspire?
4. What new perspectives or experiences shed light on it?
5. What step-by-step process solves it, in my own words?
6. What quotes, stories, studies or numbers support it?

Do not answer for me. If my answer is vague, ask a sharper follow-up question before moving on. At the end, summarise my answers as an outline.

8Applied: a market analysis

Koe ends with a YouTube workflow: one prompt each for the title, key points, introduction, script, b-roll and description, each feeding the next. The same chain works for analytical work. Here it is applied to the kind of weekly market analysis on this site.

Each step is built with the method above. For the competitor step, for example, take a well-written equity research note (route 4), have the AI break down how it profiles a company, and use the metaprompt to turn that into a prompt that first asks which market and company you are analysing.

Prompts for each step 
I am writing a market analysis of [market] in [country] for [audience]. Ask me one question at a time until you know the purpose, the time frame and what decision the reader faces. Then propose the single question the analysis should answer, and three sub-questions.
For [market], find: market size, growth over the last five years, and the market shares of the main players. Give a source and date for every number. If two sources disagree, show both. Do not estimate a number without saying it is an estimate.

My take In analysis, the verify step is not optional. Koe says narrowing the context prevents hallucination. It reduces it, but it does not remove it. My own rule, from building this site, is to fetch every important number twice, in two different forms, and check one against the other. The agents behind this site work the same way.

9What holds up, and what doesn't

Holds up

  • Context beats cleverness. Most poor output comes from a poor brief.
  • Letting the AI interview you is the fastest way to give it context.
  • Learning from strong examples and experts sets a higher standard than the default.
  • A library of tested prompts turns one-off wins into a process.
  • Coaching prompts that help you think age better than prompts that think for you.

Needs a caveat

  • Longer is not better in itself. Relevant context helps; filler dilutes it.
  • Narrow context reduces errors but does not prevent them. Check facts and numbers.
  • Model recommendations from late 2025 are already dated.
  • Saved instructions in projects now do much of what pasted prompts did.
  • The «99%» in the title is marketing, and the episode also sells software.

10Checklist

  1. Pick a task you do often.
  2. Get expert instructions for it: write them, ask for a guide, use an expert source or break down an example.
  3. Open a new chat with the metaprompt and paste the instructions.
  4. Make sure the prompt asks you questions first, then plans, then drafts.
  5. Run it in a fresh chat. Note what went wrong.
  6. Improve the prompt and save it in your library.
  7. For anything factual, add a verify step.
