Buy yesterday’s loser, short yesterday’s winner

A simple daily strategy on Oslo Børs: every morning, buy the OSEBX stock that fell most yesterday and short the one that rose most. Does betting on a bounce back pay, and does it matter when you close the trade?

The rules

  1. Day 0, after the close. Rank the OSEBX stocks by their return that day. The biggest loser and the biggest winner are picked.
  2. Day 1, at the open. Put half the money into the loser and short the winner with the other half.
  3. Close the trade in one of three ways, compared side by side:
    • 1 at the close the same day (held during the day only),
    • 2 at the open the next day (held overnight),
    • 3 at the close five trading days later (held for a week).

Only liquid stocks are used: a median daily turnover of at least NOK 5 million over the previous three months and a share price of at least NOK 1. Prices are adjusted for dividends and splits.

Trading costs

Return a year is compounded. Volatility and Sharpe ratio are annualised from daily changes, with a zero risk-free rate. Hit rate is the share of trades that made money after costs. OSEBX is buy and hold, without costs.

What the backtest leaves out

  • Shorting is not free. Borrowing shares costs a fee, and the most volatile Oslo stocks are often hard to borrow. No borrowing fee is included.
  • Opening prices. The test assumes you get the official opening price. In practice the opening auction and the spread in small stocks cost extra.
  • One stock per side. Results depend on a handful of very large moves, so a few trades decide the outcome.
  • Survivorship. Only companies still listed today have data, the same limitation as in the risk project.

This is a backtest for learning, not investment advice. Past results say little about future results, and short selling can lose more than the money put in.
