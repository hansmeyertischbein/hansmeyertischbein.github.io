      Does the plan add up?

        How it works

        Each task gets a priority index:

        P = (V ÷ p) × e−max(A − p, 0) ÷ H

        V is what's at stake if the task never gets done, on a scale where the steps grow: 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13 and 21. p is your time estimate in hours, multiplied by the estimate correction because most of us underestimate. A is the plannable time until the deadline, so A − p is the slack. H, the urgency horizon, is two days of plannable time.

        The first part ranks by value per hour, which is the order that minimises the total cost of tasks waiting (Smith's rule). The second part is an urgency factor between 0 and 1 that rises as slack runs out, borrowed from the ATC rule in production scheduling. Urgency is calculated from your deadlines instead of how pressing a task feels, because people reliably overrate urgent but unimportant tasks.

        The feasibility check sums the estimated time in deadline order and compares it with the time you have at each deadline. If every deadline can be met, this order will meet them.

        A few rules from the paper are built in. Give every task a deadline, even a self-imposed one, or important work without a deadline never rises. Split tasks bigger than a day into parts with their own deadlines. Plan at most 80% of your time. Work on no more than three tasks at once, and book fixed blocks for valuable tasks that aren't urgent yet. Put small recurring tasks like email in a daily batch instead of the ranking, and do anything that takes under two minutes straight away.
